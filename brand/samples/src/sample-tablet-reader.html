Walking the long valley: notes on slowness

Four days on foot, no fixed plan, and what happens to your attention when the next town is a whole afternoon away.

I
Ines · September 21, 2026

The first morning, I kept checking the time. Not because I needed to be anywhere, but because the habit had nowhere else to go. By the second day the checking had slowed, and by the third I noticed that the valley had its own clock: the light moving across the far ridge, the bell from a farm I never saw.

Slowness is usually sold as a luxury, something you buy with a week off and a nice hotel. On foot it feels more like a correction. Distances return to their real size.
